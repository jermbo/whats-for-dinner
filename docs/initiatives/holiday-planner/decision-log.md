# Decision log: holiday planner

For a holiday you cook many dishes, and other people bring some of them. You want to plan the menu, and you want to know who brings what. The weekly [Plan](../../architecture/plan.md) holds one recipe for each meal, and it cannot tell that Mum brings the pie. This page records each decision for a planner of one [occasion](glossary.md).

## What the plugin does today

| Question                                 | The plugin today                                                                        |
| ---------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Can a meal have more than one dish?      | Yes. A meal is a main dish and its sides: one task of the [plan note](../../architecture/plan-note.md), with one indented line for each side. |
| Can a dish come from a different person? | No.                                                                                     |
| Can a recipe change its amounts?         | No. The plugin has no scale. The yield of a recipe is free text, such as "4 servings".  |
| What goes on the list?                   | Each ingredient of each meal that you did not cook, and of each side that is a recipe, minus the pantry. |
| How many parts has the kitchen?          | Four: Pantry, Recipes, Plan, and Shop.                                                  |

## Decisions

1. **The holiday planner is a screen of its own, not a part of the weekly Plan.** The reason: the owner tried an occasion inside the Plan in two prototypes of 21 October, and the [meal plan](../meal-plan/decision-log.md) log records why it did not convince. Holiday planning is a different concern.
2. **An occasion is one note.** The note holds the full plan of the occasion in Markdown, so you can read it and change it with no plugin.
3. **An occasion is not from a list of holidays.** You give it a name and a day, such as "Sam's birthday" on a Saturday, or Thanksgiving. The reason: the owner also cooks for a birthday, or for no reason.
4. **The plugin has a place for the occasions.** The screen must be pleasant to look at, and easy to understand.
5. **The screen of an occasion shows who brings what, the menu, what to buy, and the tasks (decision 30).** "Who brings" has one place card for you, one for each guest with a job, and one for the open jobs. The menu has one card for each course. The reason: in the first prototype, the owner liked the page that put the dishes of each person together. In the third prototype, the owner wanted the menu by course as well, as a sign-up sheet. An option that was rejected: only groups by course, with the person on each dish.
6. **The occasion notes are in the occasions folder: `Larder/Occasions`.** It is a setting, as the shopping folder is. The name of the note is the name of the occasion, such as `Thanksgiving 2026.md`. The reason: each note of the plugin goes into a folder that a setting names, inside the plugin home.
7. **The day of the occasion is the property `date`**, as year-month-day: `date: 2026-11-26`. The reason: a property is the place for one fact of a full note, and Obsidian shows it in a calendar view.
8. **A dish is a recipe or only a name.** "Pumpkin pie" that Mum brings needs no recipe. The reason: you do not keep the recipes of other people.
9. **The person of a dish is plain text, such as "Mum". A dish with no person is a dish that you cook. A dish with the person `?` is open: nobody brings it yet (decision 29).** A link such as `[[Mum]]` also works, and the screen shows its name. The reason: most dishes are yours, so you write a person only for the other dishes.
10. **The course of a dish is a free word.** With no course, a recipe gives its [type](../../architecture/type.md), and a name with no recipe goes into "Other". The screen puts the known courses first: starter, main, side, dessert, drink. The reason: the type of a recipe is usually correct, and a word of your own, such as "bread", must also work.
11. **Only the dishes that you cook go on the list.** A dish of a different person never puts food on the list. The reason: Mum buys her own pumpkins.
12. **The pantry is one pantry.** The list adds the food of the occasion to the food of the week, and then takes off the pantry, as for two meals. A row of the list names the occasion as its reason, as a row names its meal. The reason: the week and the occasion take food from the same shelves.
13. **A dish that you cook has "Cook", and Cook Mode does not change.** After the meal, Cook Mode writes the record in the recipe and updates the pantry, as for a meal of the week. "Cook" on a dish does not change the weekly plan, also when the same recipe is a meal of the week. The reason: the end of a meal works, and an occasion needs nothing different there. The week and the occasion stay apart (decision 16).
14. **A task that is done is a dish that is ready: you cooked it, or it came.** Each dish has a check, and a tap on it gives the dish its mark or takes the mark off. A dish that you cooked takes its food off the list. The task stays in the note with its mark, so the note of the occasion is the record of the day. The reason: you see at one look what is ready. This is not the rule of the weekly plan, where a cooked meal leaves the note (see the [meal plan](../meal-plan/decision-log.md) log).
15. **With two active occasions, the nearest one is "Next up", and the other is a row below it (decision 27).** The reason: the Occasions part shows each occasion, so a second one needs no chip.
16. **The weekly Plan does not show the occasion.** The occasion does not take a place of the week, and "New occasion" is in the Occasions part (decision 21). The reason: the [meal plan](../meal-plan/decision-log.md) initiative changes the Plan, and the two concerns stay apart.
17. **A dish is one task line, with its fields after a dot, as in the plan note.** The fields are the course, the scale, and the person, each one optional:

    ```markdown
    ---
    date: 2026-11-26
    ---
    - [ ] [[Recipes/Roast turkey|Roast turkey]] · main
    - [ ] [[Recipes/Green beans|Green beans]] · side · ×2
    - [ ] Pumpkin pie · dessert · Mum
    - [x] Rolls · bread · Sam

    ## To do

    - [ ] Borrow chairs · 2026-11-24
    ```

    A field of the form `×3` is the scale, and the words `prep done` are the preparation (decision 24). Of the other fields, the first is the course and the second is the person. So in `Rolls · Sam`, "Sam" is the course. When a dish has a person, the screen also writes its course: the course that the screen shows for it.

    The reason: you know the form from the plan note, one line is one dish, and a new person for a dish is a change of one word. A fixed sequence needs no new word in the line. Options that were rejected: one heading for each person, because a new person moves the line; one table, because it has no check box; and a word before the person, such as `· by Mum`.
18. **A dish has a scale, such as `×3`, in its line.** The list uses the scale. Cook Mode shows "×3" at the top, and the amounts of its steps stay as the recipe has them. The last page of Cook Mode takes the amounts times the scale out of the pantry. With no scale, it is `×1`. The reason: you know that a pie for 8 is enough for 12, and the plugin does not. You used three times the food, so the pantry must lose it. Options that were rejected: no scale; and a number of guests that the plugin divides by the yield, because the yield is free text.
19. **An occasion is active from the moment that its note exists, up to the day after its date.** A note with no `date` is active, with no end. The reason: you made the note on purpose, and you never have to remember a status. The day after is there for the leftovers and the dishes that you did not mark. Options that were rejected: a window of 14 days before the date; and a property `status: active`.
20. **The kitchen has a fifth part, "Occasions", at all times.** It lists each occasion note (decision 27), and a tap on an occasion opens its screen. The days of an occasion are "today" on the date, "1 day" and "5 days" before it, and "yesterday" on the day after. A note with no date shows only its name. The reason: the owner tried the first build on the phone, and could not find how to add an occasion, where the notes are, or how to see a past one. A part that shows only while an occasion is active hides all three. Options that were rejected: a part that shows only while an occasion is active, with its name and its days in the nav; a card at the top of the Plan; and a card at the top of each part.
21. **You start an occasion with "New occasion" in the Occasions part, or with an Obsidian command.** "New occasion" is a wide button below the head of the part (decision 27). The form asks for a name and a day. A day before today, or a name that an occasion has, does not start an occasion. The reason: the Occasions part is always there, so the button is where the occasions are. The Plan keeps only "Add a meal". An occasion with a past day is over before you plan it. Options that were rejected: a link button in the head of the Plan part; only the command; and a note by hand.
22. **Your dishes go on the list 7 days before the date.** Before that, the screen of the occasion shows what to buy, and "Add to the list" on a row puts it on the list earlier. "Add to the list" adds the food item as an extra, as "Add to the list" in the Pantry part does. A note with no date puts food on the list only with "Add to the list". 7 days is a fixed rule, not a setting. The reason: a plan for Thanksgiving in October must not put a turkey on each list for 5 weeks. The list already knows an extra, so an early row needs no new form. Options that were rejected: on the list at once; and only after a tap on "Put on the list".
23. **The screen of the occasion changes the note.** You add a dish from the recipes or by name, in the card of a person or of a course. A tap on a dish opens "Who brings?" (decision 31), where you give it a person, a course, and a scale, or remove it. As you type a dish, the field proposes the recipes that the name finds, and a recipe that is a dish already is not proposed. A name with no recipe adds a dish with only that name. The scales are ×1, ×1.5, ×2, ×3, and ×4. The reason: you plan on the phone, and to type the form of a line on a phone is slow. The field "Add an item" of the Shop works the same way. Options that were rejected: a screen that only shows the note; "Add a dish" that opens the Recipes part; and a row of small chips below the dish.
24. **The screen shows the preparation of each of your dishes with "Prep is done", and your occasion tasks in the sequence of their days.** The occasion tasks are the task lines below the heading `## To do`, such as `- [ ] Borrow chairs · 2026-11-24`. A task with no day comes last. "Prep is done" writes the words `prep done` in the line of the dish, as in the plan note. A tap on a task gives it its mark. "Add a task" below the tasks adds a task with no day. You give a task a day in the note. The reason: the work for a holiday is more than food, and a task is plain Markdown. The heading tells a task from a dish with no recipe, such as "Pumpkin pie". The owner used "Add a task" in each prototype. An option that was rejected: a timeline that the plugin calculates back from the time that you eat, because a recipe has no time.
25. **After the day, the occasion goes to "Past" in the Occasions part, and the note stays as it is.** The plugin never deletes an occasion note. To delete one, you delete the note in Obsidian. The reason: the note is the record of the occasion, and a past occasion is where the next one starts (decision 26).
26. **"New occasion" can start from a past occasion.** It copies the dishes, the persons, the scales, and the occasion tasks with no day into a new note, with a new name and a new date. Each task of the copy has no mark, and no dish has `prep done`. The form shows the 5 newest past occasions as chips. The reason: next Thanksgiving starts from this Thanksgiving. The work of last year is not the work of this year. An option that was rejected: a review with a rating for each dish on the day after, because Cook Mode already asks for a rating of each dish that you cook.
27. **The Occasions part puts the next occasion first, as one large card, and each other occasion is a short row below it.** From the top: the head "Occasions", a wide button "New occasion", the card "Next up", one row of chips, and the rows. The card "Next up" is the nearest occasion that is not past. It shows the name, the date, the days, and four numbers: the people who come, the open jobs, the food to buy, and the tasks to do. These are the numbers at the top of the occasion (decision 30), so the list and the occasion read as one thing. A row shows the name, the date, the number of dishes, the number of people, and the days. The chips are the filters "Upcoming", "Past", and "All", then the sequences "Nearest first", "Newest first", and "A to Z". The part has no find field. The reason: the owner compared three layouts in a prototype on 31 October, and selected this one. An occasion is rare, so you usually look at only the next one, and the list is short. Options that were rejected: a grid of cards with the days as a large number, and the find field and filters of the Recipes part; and a calendar with one group for each month and a date stamp on each row.
28. **An occasion has a guest list: the heading `## Guests`, with one line for each guest, such as `- Mum` or `- The Smiths ×4`.** The number after `×` is the party size, and a line with no number is 1. You count as 1. "N coming" is you and each party size. A person of a dish who is not on the list is a guest with the size 1, and the screen writes that guest into the list when you change the size. A copy of a past occasion gets its guest list (decision 26). The reason: most people only come, and you want to know how many people come in total. A line of a list reads well in Obsidian, and a property cannot hold a party size well. Options that were rejected: a property `people`; one number of guests for the whole occasion; and labels such as "cook" or "visitor" that you give each guest.
29. **A job is a thing that a person brings: a dish, a course with no dish yet, such as "Dessert", or a thing that is not food, such as "Plates".** A job is a dish line. A guest with no job is a visitor. A job with the person `?` is open, and the screen shows "Who brings?". A thing that is not food has the course `things`, and it puts nothing on the list, as each name with no recipe. When you remove a guest, each job of that guest becomes open. The reason: the role of a guest is what the guest brings, so there is nothing more to keep current. An open job is a slot of a sign-up sheet. Options that were rejected: separate role labels; and things that are not food in "To do".
30. **The screen of an occasion is one page: the ink card, "Who brings" with the tab "Guest list", the menu, "To buy", and "To do".** The ink card is the card "Next up" of the list (decision 27): the date, the days, the name, "⋯", and the four numbers. A number is a button that scrolls to its section, and "coming" opens the guest list. "Who brings" has a place card for you, one for each guest with a job, and one for the open jobs: the name above a fold line, and below it the jobs. The guest list has one short row for each guest: the name, the jobs or "Visitor", the party size with − and +, and ✕. The menu has one card for each known course and for each course of your own, also with no dish: an empty course is a dashed card, such as "No dessert yet", with "Add a dessert". A dish in the menu shows a sticker of its person. "← Occasions" goes back to the list. The reason: the owner compared six layouts in three prototype rounds on 31 October, and selected this one. A card for each guest made the screen full, because most guests have no job. Options that were rejected: the screen of the first build with a row of guests; four tabs; one card for each guest; and a board where you drag a job to a guest.
31. **A tap on a dish opens "Who brings?": a sheet from the bottom of the screen.** It has one large button for you, for each guest, and for "Nobody yet", a field "New person" that adds a guest, the courses, the scales, and "Remove dish". The reason: a large button is one tap for the thumb. An option that was rejected: a row of small chips below the dish.
32. **"⋯" on the ink card has "Open the note" and "Delete occasion".** "Delete occasion" asks first, and then moves the note to the trash, as Obsidian does with a note that you delete. The reason: the owner could not find how to remove an occasion. A note in the trash can come back.
